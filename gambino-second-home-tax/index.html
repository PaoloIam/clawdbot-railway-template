Annual Tax

New York City has adopted an annual surcharge on high-value residences that are not a primary home — often called the pied-à-terre tax. Get the facts, then estimate what a property might owe each year.

The Facts

What is the Second Home Annual Tax?

A recurring, yearly surcharge layered on top of existing NYC property taxes for qualifying residential properties used as a second home rather than a primary residence.

$

Who it affects

Owners of NYC homes valued at $5 million or more that are not occupied as the owner's primary residence — typically buyers who live outside the five boroughs.

%

How much

A progressive surcharge on value above the threshold — starting around 0.5% and scaling to roughly 4% on the highest-value homes — charged every year.

🏠

Key exemptions

Properties used as the primary residence of the owner (or an immediate family member), and homes under a bona-fide long-term lease (1+ year) where the tenant lives there full-time.

↺

It recurs

Unlike the one-time mansion tax paid at closing, this is an annual obligation for as long as the property remains a qualifying second home.

How it stacks up. The Second Home Annual Tax is in addition to your ordinary NYC property tax bill and any transfer/mansion taxes paid at purchase. The estimate below covers only the annual second-home surcharge — not your base property tax.
